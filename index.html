01 — AI research terminal · Flagship

finplatform. Research that tells you the truth.

TradingView’s charts, CNBC’s news, and an options calculator, in one place and free. Ask it a question in plain English and it answers from live market data.

5→1five finance apps I had built, merged into one
freeno paywall on the research surfaces
plain Englishask a question, get the numbers behind the answer

I kept building the same thing in pieces. A screener here, an options tool there, an earnings digest, a research assistant. Each one solved a slice and none of them talked to each other, so using them meant four tabs and a lot of copying numbers between them.

finplatform is those pieces merged. Charts you can actually read, the news that moves a name, an options panel that turns a live chain into plain-English odds, and a screener where adding one metric gives you a filter, a sort and a column for free. The AI sits on top rather than in the way: you ask what you want to know, and it answers from the same live data the charts are drawn from.

The part I care about most is that it will tell you when it does not know. Every answer is checked against a set of rules before you see it, and if the data is not there it says so instead of filling the gap.

Python · React + Vite · Ollama on RTX 5090 · Claude API · Supabase · GitHub Actions

02 — Translation workspace

Passage. Translation that shows its work.

Translate a document, a photo, or your voice, and see every line next to the original so you can check it.

4ways in: type it, drop a file, photograph it, say it
side by sideevery segment sits next to its source
96.86%cheaper than the agency quote it replaced

You paste a page of Portuguese and get back a page of English. The question you cannot answer is whether the third paragraph is right. Passage puts every segment next to its source, so you can check one line, fix it, and keep going rather than trusting the whole thing at once.

Point your camera at a street sign and the translation is painted back onto the photo. Speak and it speaks back. And when a translation fails it says so, rather than handing your own sentence back and calling it done.

03 — Real estate · Deal Lens

Deal Lens. Know what a property is worth before you bid.

I invest in St. Louis tax-sale real estate. Deal Lens is the underwriting tool I wished existed on my first bid.

The list is public. The tooling is a PDF.

Every year the City of St. Louis auctions tax-delinquent properties through the Sheriff’s land tax sale. You get a list of addresses and an opening bid. What you do not get is any way to tell which of them are worth showing up for.

Deal Lens takes the whole sale, maps it, and underwrites each property: comparable sales, a bid ceiling to stop at, what it will cost to clear title, and the odds the owner redeems before you ever take possession. Estimates are labelled as estimates, so you can see which numbers need a direct pull before you trust them.

MAGE — an AI coach for people practising alone

Coaching works because someone tells you the one thing to change while you can still feel the shot you just took. Film yourself instead and the insight arrives that evening, too late to be worth much. MAGE closes that gap: upload one clip, get an AR overlay drawn on the frame where your elbow went wrong. Designed end to end through paper roleplay with people mid-practice, a click-through prototype, and a user test that changed the build.
