Design exercise

Build your visual vocabulary

Draw an alphabet of basic shapes. Use it to build words. Then use grammar to relate them into a sentence. Mine came out as: I saw a rocket ship launch on a trip to Texas.

David Neish

Three steps, twenty minutes, a sharpie and a sheet of paper. The version below is mine. Try your own.

01Alphabet

Draw your basic shapes and lines. Mine came out to six, and nothing below uses anything else. Click one to see everywhere it is used.

click a shape to light up every place it appears

02Words

Use the alphabet to build words. A word is a person, a thing, a single idea. Mine are three nouns: the fins make the rocket a rocket rather than a tube, and Texas is a hat.

03Grammar

Grammar is how you relate the words: placement on the page, a connecting line, a change in angle. I used two rules. A dashed line means something is being seen, and a tilt means motion, so Texas reads as the ground it launched from rather than the thing being looked at.

Dashed line: something seen, not something touched. Tilted rocket: a launch, not a rocket sitting on a pad.

Notes

The challenging aspect of this was working backwards. I knew the sentence straight away, but reverse engineering an alphabet that could actually spell it took longer, especially fitting a specific place like Texas into a few marks.

What stuck with me is how far a small set of shapes goes once the rules are clear.
