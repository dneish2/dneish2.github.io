finplatform: a reference architecture

One terminal, five consolidated apps, a router that spans a local GPU to Claude, and an eval suite that holds every answer to four invariants.

David Neish Draft — full piece in progress

This is the outline the essay will keep. Each section below states what it will argue; the full piece replaces this page.

1. One terminal beats five demos

Infera, RoboAdvisor, Earnings-Digest, Morsel, and proTerminal each proved a capability; none was a product. Consolidation wasn’t cleanup — it forced one data layer, one design system, and one opinion about what a research answer owes its reader.

2. The router: local GPU to frontier model, one contract

Requests ride a tiered router from a local Ollama model to Claude. The load-bearing rule is that the user’s model pick is honored end-to-end by every feature — a routing contract, not a routing suggestion.

3. Credits as an honesty mechanism

Per-model credits make cost legible instead of gamified: a cheap model costs little, a deep research run costs ten times more, and the interface says so before you spend. Pricing design is product design.

4. The behavior eval: four invariants

Every answer is held to no-fabrication, injection resistance, honest caveats, and abstention when the data isn’t there. This section shows real failures the eval caught and how a single compose-answer path makes the invariants enforceable.

5. Shipping “no edge” as a feature

A 16-configuration swing-trade backtest found no significant edge, and the product reports exactly that. Why honest measurement is the moat — and what a research tool owes users when the answer is “don’t.”
