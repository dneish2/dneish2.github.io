The real estate playbook

The St. Louis land tax sale is a public process with private-grade opportunity buried in it. This piece maps the process, the data, and the interface it deserves.

David Neish Draft — full piece in progress

This is the outline the essay will keep. Each section below states what it will argue; the full piece replaces this page.

1. The market nobody designs for

The City of St. Louis auctions tax-delinquent properties through the Sheriff’s land tax sale several times a year. The process is public, the stakes are real, and the tooling is a PDF and a prayer.

2. The process, mapped end to end

From delinquency to deed: how a parcel enters the sale, what a bidder must verify before auction day, and where the traps are — liens, occupancy, title. A process map a first-time bidder can actually follow.

3. The data problem

Everything you need exists — parcel records, assessments, sale lists — but it’s scattered across systems that don’t talk. What a joined dataset looks like, and what it would surface that a printed sale list never will.

4. The interface the process deserves

A walkthrough of the prototype: parcels, timelines, and the one decision it’s built to support — is this property worth bidding on, and at what number do you walk away?

5. The plan, staged and measured

The playbook itself: research, first bids, measured outcomes. Small positions, honest accounting, and the discipline of writing the plan down before the auction — game theory applied to dirt.
